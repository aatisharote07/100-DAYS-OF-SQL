# Day 100: SQL Challenge - Advanced Analytics (Customer Lifetime Value, RFM Segmentation & Cohort Performance)

## 📌 Business Scenario
🏆 **CONGRATULATIONS ON REACHING DAY 100 OF THE 100 DAYS OF SQL CHALLENGE!** 🏆

To celebrate the grand finale, Day 100 tackles the ultimate capstone problem in enterprise analytics: **The Unified Customer 360 & RFM Segmentation Pipeline**.

The executive leadership team at a high-growth subscription & direct-to-consumer platform is reviewing end-of-year performance. Rather than viewing isolated sales totals, they require a comprehensive Customer 360 summary that evaluates:
1. **Recency ($R$)**: How many days have elapsed since each customer's most recent purchase (relative to the snapshot date `'2026-11-20'`)?
2. **Frequency ($F$)**: The total count of completed, non-refunded orders placed by the customer.
3. **Monetary Value ($M$)**: The total cumulative net revenue generated by the customer.
4. **RFM Tier Scoring**: Dividing customers into relative performance tiers ($1$ to $4$) across Recency, Frequency, and Monetary dimensions using windowed quartiles (`NTILE(4)`).
5. **Customer Classification**: Assigning human-readable persona tags (e.g., `'Champions'`, `'Loyal Customers'`, `'At Risk'`, or `'Hibernating'`) based on combined composite scores.

This challenge integrates Common Table Expressions (CTEs), multi-table Joins with conditional exclusions, aggregate analytics, and multi-dimensional window ranking into a single end-to-end analytical pipeline.

## ❓ The Question
Write a comprehensive SQL query evaluating all customers as of `'2026-11-20'`.

1. **Calculate Base RFM Metrics**:
   - `recency_days`: Days between `'2026-11-20'` and their most recent `'COMPLETED'` order. If a customer has no completed orders, set recency to `999`.
   - `frequency`: Total count of `'COMPLETED'` orders placed (default `0`).
   - `monetary_value`: Total sum of `net_amount` across `'COMPLETED'` orders (default `0.00`).
2. **Assign Relative RFM Quartiles (1 to 4)** using `NTILE(4)`:
   - `r_score`: Quartile based on `recency_days ASC` (lower recency days = more recent = higher score, or rank ascending).
   - `f_score`: Quartile based on `frequency ASC` (higher frequency = higher score).
   - `m_score`: Quartile based on `monetary_value ASC` (higher spend = higher score).
3. **Assign a Customer Segment**:
   - `'Champions'`: When `r_score >= 3` AND `f_score >= 3` AND `m_score >= 3`.
   - `'Loyal Customers'`: When `f_score >= 3` AND `m_score >= 2`.
   - `'Recent Buyers'`: When `r_score >= 3` AND `f_score < 3`.
   - `'At Risk / Inactive'`: All other customers.

Return `customer_id`, `customer_name`, `recency_days`, `frequency`, `monetary_value`, `r_score`, `f_score`, `m_score`, and `customer_segment`. Order by `m_score DESC`, `frequency DESC`, `recency_days ASC`.

## 💡 The Solution

```sql
WITH CustomerAggregates AS (
    -- Step 1: Compute raw RFM metrics per customer
    SELECT 
        c.customer_id,
        c.customer_name,
        COALESCE(DATEDIFF('2026-11-20', MAX(o.order_date)), 999) AS recency_days,
        COUNT(o.order_id) AS frequency,
        COALESCE(SUM(o.net_amount), 0.00) AS monetary_value
    FROM customers c
    LEFT JOIN orders o 
        ON c.customer_id = o.customer_id 
        AND o.status = 'COMPLETED'
    GROUP BY 
        c.customer_id, 
        c.customer_name
),
RFMScores AS (
    -- Step 2: Bin customers into quartiles (1-4) across Recency, Frequency, and Monetary dimensions
    SELECT 
        customer_id,
        customer_name,
        recency_days,
        frequency,
        monetary_value,
        -- Note: For recency, fewer days elapsed is BETTER, so we order DESC to give recent users higher scores
        NTILE(4) OVER (ORDER BY recency_days DESC) AS r_score,
        NTILE(4) OVER (ORDER BY frequency ASC) AS f_score,
        NTILE(4) OVER (ORDER BY monetary_value ASC) AS m_score
    FROM CustomerAggregates
)
-- Step 3: Classify customers into business segments based on quartile combinations
SELECT 
    customer_id,
    customer_name,
    recency_days,
    frequency,
    monetary_value,
    r_score,
    f_score,
    m_score,
    CASE 
        WHEN r_score >= 3 AND f_score >= 3 AND m_score >= 3 THEN 'Champions'
        WHEN f_score >= 3 AND m_score >= 2                  THEN 'Loyal Customers'
        WHEN r_score >= 3 AND f_score < 3                   THEN 'Recent Buyers'
        ELSE 'At Risk / Inactive'
    END AS customer_segment
FROM RFMScores
ORDER BY 
    m_score DESC, 
    frequency DESC, 
    recency_days ASC;
```

*(Note for PostgreSQL users: Replace `DATEDIFF('2026-11-20', MAX(o.order_date))` with `(DATE '2026-11-20' - MAX(o.order_date))`)*
*(Note for SQL Server users: Replace `DATEDIFF(d1, d2)` with `DATEDIFF(day, MAX(o.order_date), '2026-11-20')`)*

## 📝 Explanation

### The Architecture of RFM Customer 360
- **Step 1 (Granular Base Aggregation)**:
  - We `LEFT JOIN` the dimensional `customers` table to `orders` while filtering `o.status = 'COMPLETED'` in the `ON` clause. This ensures that non-purchasing users (or users with only cancelled/refunded orders like George Clark) are retained with `$0.00` and `frequency = 0`.
  - `COALESCE(DATEDIFF(...), 999)` gracefully penalizes users who have never made a completed purchase.
- **Step 2 (Statistical Distribution with `NTILE`)**:
  - `NTILE(4)` partitions the ordered customer base into 4 equal buckets (quartiles).
  - **Recency Directionality**: Because a smaller recency number is superior (bought yesterday vs. bought 8 months ago), `ORDER BY recency_days DESC` assigns bucket `4` to the most recent buyers and bucket `1` to the longest-dormant buyers.
  - **Frequency & Monetary**: High order counts and high revenue are superior, so standard `ORDER BY ASC` correctly assigns bucket `4` to the top spenders and power buyers.
- **Step 3 (Persona Segmentation)**:
  - High scores across all three dimensions identify `'Champions'` (e.g., Charlie and Alice).
  - Users with strong repeat purchase frequency but moderate monetary value are identified as `'Loyal Customers'`.
  - Recent single-purchase buyers are categorized as `'Recent Buyers'` to trigger cross-sell campaigns.
  - All remaining dormant or low-engagement accounts are marked as `'At Risk / Inactive'` for re-engagement or win-back outreach.
